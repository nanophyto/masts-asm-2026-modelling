Getting started with modelling

MASTS Annual Science Meeting · 12 November 2026

This workshop introduces two approaches to marine biogeochemistry:

  • Mechanistic models: OceanBioME.jl and Oceananigans.jl represent plankton growth, grazing, nutrient uptake and particle sinking. Begin with a single reference column before changing biological parameters.
  • Data-driven models: Abil.py is available for the companion Python exercises.

Workshop workstation

Open the personal workstation link provided by the instructor and sign in with your assigned nanophyto.org account. The editor starts inside the participant-friendly workshop/ folder. See Getting started for Julia REPL and figure-viewing instructions.

Workshop walkthrough

  1. Run a reference column: Build a LOBSTER water column using the real OceanBioME/Oceananigans constructors, run the simulation and inspect its plots of vertical ecology, nitrogen partitioning, living versus detrital particles, and carbon export.
  2. Change the grazing rate: Compare control and reduced-grazing experiments, their phytoplankton/zooplankton balance, and interpret the effect on plankton and export at 100 m and 300 m, and compare attenuation over 50–500 m with the Martin curve.
  3. Running the examples on your own system: Set up Julia and Python on a laptop and reproduce the exercises after the workshop.

The physical-forcing appendix explains the prescribed light and vertical mixing in the reference column and will compare the BATS, HOT, NABE and DYFAMED station forcings as they are added.
